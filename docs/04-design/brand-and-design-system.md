# Brand & Design System Guide

## 1. Brand

**The Racket Lifestyle**

Core line:
**Gear and lifestyle for people who live the sport.**

The supplied The Tennis Life style guide is the source inspiration. It describes a confident, warm, sharp, aspirational-but-grounded personality and emphasizes real courts, off-court life, editorial composition and a knowledgeable club-member voice.

This project broadens that tennis-first framework to racket sports.

## 2. Creative direction

The new system should feel:
- premium — considered, unhurried, never discount-coded
- brighter
- fresher
- more lifestyle-led
- slightly more fashion/editorial
- gender-inclusive
- especially inviting to women
- still performance credible

Avoid:
- dark masculine "locker room" aesthetics
- aggressive black/red sports branding
- discount marketplace clutter
- neon overload

## 3. Color palette

### Primary
- Racket Lime — #E5F52F — primary CTA, key highlights
- Court Cobalt — #006BFF — navigation, structural UI, strong links
- Play Pink — #FF5E8A — feminine-leaning warmth, editorial accents, promos
- Sky Pop — #4DD8FF — secondary accents, info states, freshness

### Supporting
- Lilac — #B7A4FF — editorial accents, collection backgrounds, subtle feminine color
- Cream — #FFFDF7 — primary page background
- Chalk — #F4F2EA — cards/sections
- Ink — #1E2430 — body copy

## 4. Usage rules

- Racket Lime should remain the main action color.
- Court Cobalt provides brand structure.
- Play Pink and Lilac give the brand a softer, more lifestyle/fashion-friendly layer.
- Sky Pop supports a brighter digital feel.
- Cream should replace large areas of stark pure white when warmth is desired.
- Do not use all four saturated colors in the same component.
- Never use saturated color as a wall of text.
- Keep Ink as the main readable text color.

## 5. Typography

The original pairing (Archivo Black + Space Grotesk + Inter + Fraunces Italic) is now the default look of template-driven and AI-assisted DTC brand kits — competent, but not distinctive next to a brand claiming "excellent taste." Revised system:

- **Fraunces** (variable — optical size and "wonk" axes pushed toward their expressive end): H1/H2, major editorial headlines. Fraunces was previously used only as a minor italic accent; promoted here to the hero display role, where its variable axes give it genuine personality that a static bold grotesque (Archivo Black) can't. Its serif warmth also reinforces the "club member, not marketplace" tone.
- **Instrument Sans**: product names, navigation, H3, labels. Replaces Space Grotesk, which has become nearly as common as Archivo Black in this exact brand category — Instrument Sans gives the same geometric-modern functional clarity without the déjà vu.
- **Inter**: body/UI. Unchanged — a workhorse typeface is not where differentiation budget should go.
- **Space Mono** (or JetBrains Mono): prices, SKUs, and technical spec data (grip size, weight, string tension, balance point). New addition, used sparingly. Racket sports are genuinely technical — this gives the brand a "precision" layer that's thematically justified rather than decorative, and is a second point of distinctiveness beyond the display font.
- Fraunces Italic no longer needs a separate role — its italic and wonk axes are available directly within the promoted display usage above.

Scale:
H1 48–64px
H2 32–40px
H3 20–24px
Nav 14–16px
Body 16px
Caption 13px
Editorial 20–28px
Spec/mono accent 13–15px

## 6. Imagery

Use:
- natural light
- real court textures
- tennis, badminton and padel contexts specifically (see PRD for sport scope)
- people actually wearing the gear and playing — not staged product-only shots
- fashion/lifestyle moments
- racket bags
- travel
- café/post-match
- social club settings
- a genuine mix of gender and race across the full image set, rotated deliberately rather than clustered by role (e.g. not "women only in soft lifestyle shots, men only in action shots")

**Photography sourcing:** primarily AI-generated (Midjourney v7 or Flux 1.1 Pro/Ultra) for posed lifestyle, candid, and product-in-use shots, where current models are strong; supplemented with a small number of real commissioned shots (even a half-day shoot at an actual Lagos court) to anchor the set, since genuine mid-action motion (racket/paddle contact, swing blur) is still the weakest spot in current AI image models and is better sourced from real photography or licensed stock. Apply a consistent warm cream + cobalt color grade across the full set so AI-generated and real images read as one cohesive shoot.

## 7. Components

Primary button:
Racket Lime background + Ink text.

Secondary:
Cream/white or transparent with Cobalt border.

Product cards:
- rounded but not childish
- large photography
- clean typography
- color accents only where needed
- strong price hierarchy
- subtle hover movement

Collection cards:
Use full-bleed photography with Cobalt/Ink overlays and Lime/Pink micro-accents.

## 8. Brand motif

Retain the ball-seam curve from the source guide as a recurring device, but make it a broader "racket orbit" motif suitable for all racket sports.

Use:
- dividers
- decorative lines
- hover states
- editorial framing
- section transitions

## 9. Responsive

Mobile-first.
Desktop should feel editorial and spacious.
Mobile should preserve the visual hierarchy without cramming content.
